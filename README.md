## What to Actually Check Before You Buy Bulk Gmail Accounts

Most comparison articles list "verified, PVA, aged, fresh" as if those words mean the same thing everywhere. They don't. Here's what each one really means and why it changes the price.

### Phone-Verified (PVA) vs Non-PVA

PVA means the account has been verified with a real phone number during creation. Google uses phone verification as a trust signal, so PVA accounts survive longer, recover from challenges more easily, and are less likely to demand re-verification when you log in from a new device. Non-PVA accounts are cheaper but get locked at the first sign of unusual activity.

### Fresh vs Aged

Fresh = created recently, no history. Aged = created months or years ago, often with some natural activity baked in (a few YouTube views, a Drive file, a profile photo). Aged accounts cost more because they've already passed Google's initial scrutiny window. For ad account registration and anything tied to Google Workspace, aged is almost always worth the premium.

### Region

This is the most underrated factor. Google compares the IP you're logging in from against the IP and phone number used at registration. A mismatch doesn't always lock the account, but it raises risk scoring. If you're running US-targeted ads, buy US Gmails and log in from US IPs. If you're farming Southeast Asian social accounts, buy Indonesia, Philippines, or Vietnam Gmails.

### Delivery Format

The standard delivery format on AccountBoy is `email----password----recovery email`. The recovery email is what you use if Google challenges you with "verify your identity" — you select "confirm your recovery email" rather than SMS, and Google sends a code to that address. The recovery email itself has no password and isn't for login; it's purely a verification channel.

## The Warm-Up Routine That Keeps Bulk Gmails Alive

This is the part most "buy bulk Gmail accounts" guides skip, and it's the part that determines whether your purchase lasts a week or a year. Google's anti-abuse system is brutal on freshly transferred accounts. Skip the warm-up and you'll burn through your budget fast.

### Day 1: Login, change nothing

Install the Gmail app on a phone (not a browser, not an emulator — a real device or a clean phone farm). Log in once. Keep the IP in the same region as the account (US Gmail = US IP, Japan Gmail = Japan IP). Don't change the password. Don't touch the recovery email. Don't send anything. Just exist.

### Days 2–7: Build trust signals

Keep the device online with a stable, region-matched IP. Slowly introduce natural activity: watch a YouTube video and leave a like, upload a small file to Google Drive, set a profile photo. These signals tell Google a real human is using the account, not a bot farm.

### Day 7+: Lock down security

Now you can change the password, swap in your own recovery email and phone, and enable two-step verification. Doing this earlier risks an "unusual activity" flag because Google sees rapid credential changes from a new IP as a hijacking pattern.

### Long-term: Avoid the mass-ban trap

> The single biggest mistake bulk buyers make is logging into multiple Gmails from the same IP. Google's abuse detection correlates accounts by IP and device fingerprint. If one account in a cluster trips a flag, the others get swept up in the same wave.

The rule AccountBoy itself publishes: **no more than 3 Gmails logged in simultaneously from any single IP.** If you're running 50 accounts, you need at least 17 distinct IPs — ideally residential proxies in the matching region, not datacenter IPs that Google already distrusts.

Other hard rules from the platform's own usage guide:

- Don't use dynamic IPs that jump regions (a Seoul IP today, a Busan IP tomorrow is fine; a Seoul IP today and a New York IP tomorrow is not).
- Don't bind the same recovery phone to multiple accounts.
